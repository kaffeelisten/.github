# Code of conduct

## The short version

Be straightforward, assume good faith, and treat the people here as colleagues. That covers almost
everything.

## What is expected

- **Talk about the work, not the person.** "This query will scan the whole table" is useful.
  "This is amateur work" is not, and is also less informative.
- **Assume competence and good intent.** Most disagreements here are about missing context rather
  than about judgement.
- **Say when you are unsure.** A question asked early is cheaper than a wrong assumption carried
  into a pull request.
- **Accept a decline gracefully.** This project has a live deployment and a deliberately narrow
  scope. A good change can still be out of scope, and that is not a verdict on you.
- **Respect the data.** Repositories here are public and campus members' names are not. Never put
  real people's data, or another person's contact details, into an issue, a pull request, a commit
  or a screenshot.

## What is not acceptable

Harassment of any kind. Insults, demeaning comments, or sustained personal hostility. Unwelcome
sexual attention. Discrimination on any basis. Publishing someone's private information. Deliberate
intimidation or stalking.

This applies in issues, pull requests, commits, reviews, and anywhere else the project is
discussed.

## Reporting

Email **arudaev** through the address on his GitHub profile, or open a private thread via the
**Security** tab on the relevant repository, which is not publicly visible.

Reports are read by the two maintainers. Honest limits: this is a two-person student project
rather than a company, so there is no committee and no formal appeals process. What we can
promise is that we will read the report, take it seriously, keep it confidential, and tell you
what we are doing about it.

If a report concerns one of the maintainers, address it to the other.

## Consequences

Depending on what happened: a private word, an edited or removed comment, a declined
contribution, or a block from the organisation. We would rather have the private conversation
first, and will where that is reasonable.

## Scope

Project spaces, and public spaces when someone is representing the project.
